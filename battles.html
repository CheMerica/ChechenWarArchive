Battles & Engagements

Major battles from both Chechen Wars, in order.

Documented Conflicts

Entries are sorted by war, with dates and a brief account of each engagement.

  1. 01

    First Chechen War (1994–1996)

    On December 11, 1994, Russia sent troops into Chechnya to crush the republic's bid for independence. Chechen fighters outfought a force that vastly outnumbered them. By August 1996, Russia signed the Khasavyurt Accords and withdrew.

    • Battle of Grozny
      • Dates: December 31, 1994 – March 6, 1995
      • Russian armored columns rolled into the capital on New Year's Eve. Chechen fighters destroyed entire armored units in the streets.
      • Result: Russia took Grozny after heavy losses. The city was rubble.
    • Battle of Argun
      • Dates: January – March 1995
      • Russia attacked Chechnya's second-largest city, just east of Grozny, to clear the lowland corridor.
      • Result: Russia occupied Argun after several weeks of street fighting.
    • Battle of Shali
      • Dates: March 1995
      • Russia pushed south into the Chechen lowlands. Chechen fighters slowed the advance through Shali before pulling back.
      • Result: Russia took Shali. The war moved to the mountains.
    • Battle of Gudermes
      • Dates: March – June 1995 (first capture); November – December 1995 (recaptured and retaken)
      • Chechnya's eastern industrial hub fell to Russia in spring 1995. Chechen fighters retook it that November. Russian forces pushed them out again by December.
      • Result: Russia held the city by year's end, at significant cost.
    • Battle of Vedeno
      • Dates: May – June 1995
      • Russia sent troops into the southern mountains to take Vedeno, the highland town that had sheltered Chechen command since the 1800s.
      • Result: Russia took Vedeno. Chechen commanders scattered into the surrounding ranges.
    • Battle of Bamut
      • Dates: March 1995 – May 1996
      • A Chechen garrison in the western village of Bamut held out for over a year, sheltering in Soviet-era missile silos beneath the hillside as Russia launched assault after assault.
      • Result: Russia finally took Bamut in May 1996, on the fourteenth month of trying.
    • Operation Jihad — Second Battle of Grozny
      • Dates: August 6–22, 1996
      • Aslan Maskhadov launched a surprise offensive on August 6, hitting Russian positions across Grozny at once and surrounding roughly 7,000 Russian troops inside the city.
      • Result: Chechnya won. Russia signed the Khasavyurt Accords and left.
  2. 02

    Second Chechen War (1999–2009)

    On August 7, 1999, Shamil Basayev and Ibn al-Khattab led Islamist fighters into Dagestan. Russia used the incursion, and the apartment bombings that followed, to justify a second invasion. Troops crossed into Chechnya on August 26. Russia bombed the republic for months before sending in ground forces. By early 2000, Russia held the cities. Fighting continued as a guerrilla war for nearly a decade.

    • Invasion of Dagestan
      • Dates: August 7 – September 14, 1999
      • Basayev and Khattab led roughly 2,000 fighters across the border, seizing villages in the Botlikh and Tsumadin districts.
      • Result: Russian and Dagestani forces pushed them back after six weeks of fighting.
    • Battle of Gudermes
      • Dates: October – November 1999
      • Local Chechen clans negotiated terms and handed the city over without a fight. Russia made Gudermes its main eastern base.
      • Result: Russia took Gudermes without major battle.
    • Battle of Grozny
      • Dates: October 1999 – February 6, 2000
      • Russia encircled Grozny and shelled it for months before sending in ground troops. Chechen fighters held each block as long as they could.
      • In the breakout on February 1, a minefield killed much of the remaining Chechen command. Field commander Lecha Dudayev died there.
      • Result: Russia took Grozny. The UN called it the most destroyed city on Earth.
    • Battle of Ulus-Kert
      • Dates: February 29 – March 1, 2000
      • A Chechen column breaking out of the Argun Gorge hit the Russian 6th Parachute Company, 84 men, on a ridge above Ulus-Kert. The paratroopers held for 18 hours. Nearly all of them died.
      • Result: 84 of 90 paratroopers died. The Chechen column broke through.
    • Siege of Komsomolskoye
      • Dates: February 29 – March 20, 2000
      • Ruslan Gelayev and roughly 1,000 fighters dug into Komsomolskoye, south of Urus-Martan, and held off encircling Russian forces for three weeks.
      • Result: Russia won. Gelayev escaped with a handful of men. Both sides took heavy casualties. No comparable engagement followed.
    • Battle of the Argun Gorge
      • Dates: February – April 2000
      • Russia swept through the Argun Gorge, the main passage Chechen fighters used to reach the southern highlands and the Georgian border.
      • Result: Russia took the gorge. Remaining fighters dispersed into the mountains and shifted to ambushes and raids.
    • Battle of Serzhen-Yurt
      • Dates: April 2000
      • Russia cleared Serzhen-Yurt in the Shali District, a village Chechen fighters had used to move supplies into the mountains.
      • Result: Russia took the village and destroyed most of it. Remaining fighters withdrew into the surrounding forests.
    • Battle of Vedeno
      • Dates: April – May 2000
      • Russia struck Vedeno again. Chechen fighters had come back to the highland district after the First War. This time Russia pushed them out and held the mountain zone.
      • Result: Russia occupied the Vedeno district. Chechen fighters kept fighting, shifting entirely to guerrilla tactics.
